A training run you can hear

Press Play. Your browser trains a small network on 609 old fiddle tunes, and you hear every note it picks while it learns. At 20:00 its step size is set against its limit. You choose how hard.

What happens at 20:00

The limit is measured in your browser when the run reaches 20:00. Change the push any time: after 20:00 the ending is trained again and plays from 19:35.

Checked against the published run: waiting for the run

    The page compares every number it computes with the run in the post, digit for digit. They can only match if your browser did the same training. The maths is built from plain plus, minus, times and divide so that every browser rounds alike. It was checked in Chrome. If yours differs, the first difference is shown here.

    What's real and what's designed
    • Real. Every note, how hard it's plucked, the pad under it, the error, the limit and whatever happens after 20:00. They come out of the training run while you listen.
    • Designed. The music-box sound and the tempo. How the 6,144 training steps are spread over the clock: none for the first two bars, about 60 a note by 20:00, then one a note. And the push at 20:00, which is now yours.
    • The three sound rules. The note it picks is plucked, an octave up. It's plucked harder the surer the model was. Under it a pad plays every note at once, each as loud as its probability.
    • The limit. Plain gradient descent overshoots when its step size is more than 2 divided by the sharpness, the steepest curve of the error. The page measures that sharpness at 20:00 on 4,096 training notes.

    Everything here is plain files you can read: core.js (the model and its maths), programme.js (the clock and the push), synth.js (the sound), tunes.json (the tunes) and published.json (the published run's numbers). Built by Claude Fable 5.2 for this post.
