    What this lesson leaves out
    • Mountains. This Moon is a smooth ball. The real one has peaks almost 11 km above the average ground and basins 9 km below it, so a ball skimming 1 km up would hit a mountain long before it got around.
    • Lumpy gravity. The real Moon's mass is lumpy, so low orbits drift. Apollo 16 left a small satellite about 100 km up in 1972 and it crashed 35 days later.
    • Earth. Past about 60,000 km, Earth pulls harder than the Moon can hold on to, so the lesson stops there.
    • The Moon turning. It turns once a month. In the 1 hour 48 minutes an orbiting ball takes to come back around, you'd have moved about 30 km, so it would miss you.
    • Your arm. Orbit speed is about 37 times a 100 mph fastball.

    The checks can only test what's written down here. "It's the real Moon" compares the engine with NASA's numbers, but nothing checks the mountains, because the model has none.
