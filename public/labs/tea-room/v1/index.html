What I'm working on this month, and the setup I use.

Updated Nov 08, 2026

Blog work and my AI-made TV series, the Wordhord idea, and the cloud setup my agents work in.

Current focus

  • This blog. It’s been my main focus lately, and I’m still improving it.
  • I Love My Evening Television. My AI-made TV series, and a demo of what Opus 5.5 can animate.

Wordhord (still an idea)

  • A shared language core. One API and database any language-learning app can read and write to track a learner’s level, streaks and vocabulary. No app has to relearn what you already know.
  • Not a translator. It isn’t any-language-to-any-language translation.
  • Vectors, not raw meaning. The core holds words as embeddings instead of raw semantics or prosody, and a closest-vector check tells it which words you’ve learned. Learn one word in a neighborhood (big, large, giant, huge, humongous) and apps can prioritize words outside it.

Current setup

Everything lives in the cloud. Each part is modular and replaceable, so the whole thing can keep expanding, and my agents work inside it.

  • Stack. Cloudflare R2 for storage, Infisical for API keys, GitHub for repos and skills, Notion as my second brain, Runpod for GPUs and Cloudflare for hosting.
  • Interface. Gages, my Grok bot, which dispatches Claude Code agents.
  • Subscriptions. Two Anthropic Max 20x plans, ChatGPT Plus and a paid xAI plan.
  • Paid APIs. ElevenLabs (AI voices) is the most important. Hugging Face is the biggest, about $57 a month, and its Seedance video model is expensive, so it’s likely the first cut. DeepSeek runs the agents inside my own code, like RAG Goobie, the Ask box on the ledger. I also have a Gemini key I probably don’t use.
  • Game dev. Godot for now. Unity and Unreal are worth another look as their MCP support (how AI agents drive them) improves, and Blender MCP for rendering deserves a second look too.

I build things with AI agents to find out where they actually hold up. Most of it is about making learning fun and open to anyone who wants it.

Most of what gets said about AI comes from people selling something. I'm not selling anything. I'd rather find out for myself, so I build real projects with it and write down what broke. That's what this site is for.

What I'm making

The big one right now is I Love My Evening Television, a series of half-hour films made by crews of Claude agents from one prompt each. Every episode starts as a calm little programme and turns strange at minute 20. The first two are on YouTube, and the lore course keeps track of the whole world.

The Hidden Curriculum is a free video course on the small stuff nobody tells you when you start building with AI, like what a folder path is. Agents write, fact-check, narrate and animate every lesson. Start it here.

Then there are my original characters. Jefrie, BloodTailor and the rest of the cast keep showing up in everything I make. They get drawn, painted in oil, animated and dropped into games.

On the game side there's BLACKWATER, zombies but you're cave diving, which I built in a weekend and you can play in your browser. And LLM Monster Hunter, a monster-catching game where no monster exists until you meet it. The AI writes, paints and referees each one while the code keeps every number. It's paused for now. Everything else is on the projects page.

Why I write it down

The ledger is where I file what I tried and what came of it, dead ends included. The prompt that seemed obviously right and wasn't. The agent that did something so strange I had to screenshot it. If you're learning this stuff too, I think an honest write-up of a failure is worth more than another launch announcement.

Away from the keyboard

I'm learning Japanese and I volunteer with a local Japanese club, including a bilingual Studio Ghibli storytime. I play fingerstyle guitar and write songs. I'm also in Toastmasters, which has done more for my writing than any style guide.
