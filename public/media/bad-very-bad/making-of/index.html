Bad. Very Bad.

The making-of, written by Claude (Opus 5.5) in Claude Code for Aaron Orelup

A Byzantine haggler tells an arms dealer his legendary sword is "bad, bad, very bad", knocks the price from 500 to 60 gold, pays like it physically hurts, and walks off deadpan. A 3D cube turn later he bursts into his home holding it over his head, grinning like a lunatic. He was lying. Pictures were made on Aaron's laptop (Qwen-Image 2.1 stills, a Blender previs, one 13-segment MiniMax-H3 latent chain), every sound came from ElevenLabs, and the edit is a frame-exact Python compositor.

What I learned

  • One shot per continuation plus an end keyframe on every segment held 13 H3 segments on one look. No drift at 1344×768, and the chain never spilled out of 8 GB.
  • H3's own audio is the lip-sync map. Its voice-band bursts are syllable-accurate, and a piecewise time map puts the ElevenLabs words on the mouths.
  • Read every strip for H3's extra cuts. Segments 9 and 10 each held two shots; a vertical step in the time map jumps across the cut.
  • A word Whisper can't read on its own is a bad take, not a mix problem. '[horrified gasp] Sixty?!' and a sulky 'Fine.' were re-voiced for 40 credits once the voices were saved. The buried 'Very bad.' was a level problem, fixed by a voice leveler and deeper ducking.
  • Put a hit on the beat by moving the cut, not the hit. The flick shot opens a 16th early so the finger touches the blade on the downbeat.
  • isnet-anime mattes are good until a busy textile sits behind a close-up. Cut it out per time range, and keep the white outline off where the matte is ragged; check outlined shots at full size.
  • When a music model ignores section lengths, build the score from parts: a 30 s groove plus a separate drop conditioned on it, cut on a measured 104.94 BPM grid.

The H3 latent chain

The haggle is ONE MiniMax-H3 latent chain (Aaron's workflows 31/32 through h3-film's film.py): every segment continues the saved AV latent of the one before, so the look, faces and light carry across the cuts. Cuts mode, 1344x768, a hard cut at about 0.9 s into each segment, every segment pinned to its keyframe. H3's own audio was discarded; its speech bursts were only used to find where the mouths move.

The raw chain, all 13 segments joined frame-exact by film.py assemble (40.6 s, with H3's own audio, which the film does not use).

Sound (ElevenLabs only)

Everything you hear came from ElevenLabs: two designed voices (Voice Design v3), three re-voiced or extra lines (v3 TTS), the haggle groove and the triumphant drop (Eleven Music), and 20 sound effects, 18 of which made the mix. The groove's beat grid (104.94 BPM) drives every cut. Whisper (medium) heard every line correctly in the final mix after two lines were re-voiced.

Cast and item

Two new characters and one prop, designed for this film. Each sheet is one Qwen-Image text-to-image job; single panels were cropped out as references so Qwen would not copy one pose into every shot.

Plates

Location plates for the market street, the stall, the reverse angle and the home. Qwen put people into two 'empty' plates and an Ottoman minaret on the 1043 Hagia Sophia; one removal edit each fixed them.

Previs

A grey-box Blender 5.2 previs of the whole film: the stall at true scale, stand-ins for Basil, Nikos and the sword, one camera per shot keyed to the beat grid of the music, cut on the real scratch mix. It fixed the shot list and timing before any video was rendered; its guide frames set the framing for the H3 end keyframes.

H3 end keyframes

One Qwen end frame per H3 continuation (the shot each segment must land on), all at 1344x768 from the character crops plus a location plate. Nikos's first 'crushed' frame read as laughing, so it was redone with the mouth corners pulled down.

The home still

The still the whole joke lands on. Seed 215 won: both arms up, the whole sword held flat over his head. For the 2.5D move it was split into a clean room plate and a Basil+sword layer.
