The clay ball

    
Five at one table, and each of them feuds with the two beside them. Drag the wire to cut the ball in two. A feud is split when the pair end up on different halves.

    
    

      

      
    

    
Put everyone on a ball, every feud as far apart as it will go, then cut anywhere: on any table, on average, the wire splits at least 87.8 % as many feuds as the best cut does (Goemans and Williamson, 1995). If Khot's Unique Games Conjecture is right (and P ≠ NP), no fast method does better.
