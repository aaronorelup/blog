[0:01] 01 Four nights, one world

I Love My Evening Television is a series of half-hour nights. In Aaron's own words, it is complete chaos connected by a calm main character, showing something creative that is good for learning. Every episode has completely new characters, and every one carries easter eggs from all the episodes before it. There are four so far. This lesson puts them on one axis, shows where each one breaks, and teaches the six marks that every line of this course wears.

  [STEEL] "This series is complete chaos connected by a calm main character who is showing something creative that is good for learning and intriguing." {Aaron series notes 2026-10-12; PROMPT_TEMPLATE}
  [STEEL] Every episode has completely new characters and is completely different from the others. {Aaron 2026-10-14}
  [STEEL] Every episode carries easter eggs from all the previous episodes: the end room as someone's room, the painting somewhere, a nod to the painted-out house, Rosie older or historic. {Aaron 2026-10-14}

[3:24] 07 What every night shares

The one exception he has set is Episode 3's planned rework, at about forty-two minutes. And in all four, the calm one's last word is Goodnight.

[4:12] 08 How sure we are

Every line in this course wears one of six marks, because Aaron asked us to keep three things apart: what is set in steel, what exists only as eggs, and our running assumptions. Steel is what Aaron has decided: Rosie is Noor's grandmother. On screen is what a made episode shows: one warm light comes back in all four. An egg is planted, and its meaning only implied: a chess machine that cheats and offers draws keeps turning up from Episode 2 on. Running is our current reading: robots and programs fill the world, and no film ever names AI. An idea is proposed, not adopted: a later episode might show the hidden house from outside, one window lit. And open means nobody has decided: whether to give Y0 a calendar year. Aaron's rule, in steel, is that anything not set in steel may change. When it does, this course redraws itself.
