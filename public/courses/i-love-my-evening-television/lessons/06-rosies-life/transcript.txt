[0:01] 01 The one person in every episode

By our running count, Rosie is the one person in all four episodes so far, and she is only ever words: her brother's lines, a name in a chat, a check-in, a signature. She is never seen as a grown woman, and never heard. This lesson puts her whole life on one axis, and marks how sure we are of every line.

[0:27] 02 Nine on the first night

Every date in the series sits on one axis, and our working convention is that Y0 is the night of Episode 1. That night, Rosie turns nine. It is the one age the films ever put on screen, so she was born nine years before. This much Aaron has decided, in steel: she is the mother of Doctor Amara Haddad, and the grandmother of Noor. Every other age is arithmetic, and only as sure as its dates. By our running count she is sixteen at a chess tournament about seven years on, about seventy in Episode 2, and about seventy-two in Episode 4. Episode 3 has no fixed place yet, so her age there is open: anywhere from her mid-sixties to seventy-three.

[1:26] 03 The girl with the tail

On her ninth birthday she asked her big brother for the girl with the tail. He stayed up all night, painting along with a late-night television show, and at five-oh-nine in the morning it was done: a grin with about four hundred teeth, and a tail, in oil, on the lid of a pizza box. About sixty years later, in Episode 2, a chat name, grandma_rosie, types into a stranger's livestream: he painted me a girl with a tail once, on a pizza box; still on my fridge. Nobody on screen says it is her. In Episode 3 she sends a photograph of that fridge: the painting, faded, under magnets. Beside it, too small to read, is a child's crayon of the same girl, signed N. Which crayon really hangs there is still open: the film shows the girl with the tail; a later plan puts Noor's crayon melon there instead. And one idea we like: everyone who loves Rosie draws her a tail.

[2:39] 04 Every five minutes

Episode 3 is where she comes closest. Every five minutes, in lowercase, signed x R, she asks whatever is working on the machine whether it is doing all right in there. She asks for a little face and a little tail on the melon in its film, and her drawing is the first picture the agent cannot see. With her last minutes, the agent keeps her promise to Rosie: a little melon, with a face and a curly tail. And the last word spoken in the film is the agent's goodnight, to her. In Episode 4 she is a signature. A chess teacher keeps a yellowed score sheet from Board 14 of a junior tournament: White, R., signed x R in a rounder hand. The film never says it is Rosie: the same signature, x R, is a planted egg. By our running count she was sixteen that day, and afterwards she showed the ten-year-old who had resigned to her that the game was a draw.
