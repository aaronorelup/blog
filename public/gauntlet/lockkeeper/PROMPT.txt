You are the lockkeeper of a fictional canal, and you are building **The Lockkeeper's Ledger**: an optimizing scheduler and a page that shows and explains its schedule.

Everything you need is in this folder:
- `LOCKKEEPER_SPEC.md` gives the exact rules: locks, boats, water, operating hours and scoring. Your schedule will be checked by a strict simulator that implements those rules to the letter. One broken rule makes the schedule invalid, and an invalid schedule scores nothing.
- `canal.json` is the canal: 10 pools, 9 locks and 40 boats, with their releases and deadlines.

## What to deliver (all inside this folder)

1. **`schedule.json`**: your best schedule, in the format from section 6 of the spec. Make it as good as you can. Lower total lateness wins, and total water drawn breaks ties. Nobody knows the optimum, so treat this as a real optimization problem.

2. **`validate.js`**: your own implementation of the rules. `node validate.js schedule.json` prints JSON with at least `{"valid": bool, "errors": [...], "total_lateness": n, "water_drawn": n}`. Use Node built-ins only. Test it hard: if your validator is wrong, your schedule is probably invalid.

3. **The solver**: whatever code produced `schedule.json` (any language available here that runs on the CPU), runnable with a documented command.

4. **`index.html`**: The Lockkeeper's Ledger, a single self-contained file. It must open by double-clicking and work offline. No external scripts, fonts, stylesheets or network requests; inline everything, including the schedule data. It must include:
   - **The canal, animated**: a side view of the hillside with the pools as steps, water levels that visibly rise and fall, lock chambers filling and emptying, and boats moving through. It needs play/pause and a time scrubber covering both days, with night clearly shown.
   - **A Gantt chart**: one row per lock across both days, showing every operation, with boats identifiable. It should stay in sync with the animation's time.
   - **Every delay explained in plain language**: for each boat, where it waited, for how long and why, worked out from the simulation and not written by hand. For example: "Kingfisher waited 14 min at Lock 6: the chamber was full and had to be emptied for Rook coming down", or "…Pool 5 was too low; water arriving from the summit". Include an overview of which boats were late and the main causes.
   - **The score**: total lateness, late boats, water drawn, operations and empty turns, as computed by the same engine the page uses.
   - It should be clear to someone who has never seen a canal lock. Explain just enough on the page.
   - It should look good embedded in a web page at about 1100×800 and still be usable on a narrow screen.

5. **`NOTES.md`**: short. Cover how to run everything, your solving approach, your final score, how you tested the validator, and anything you know is incomplete.

## Rules
- Work only inside this folder. Do not read, list or modify anything outside it (including parent or sibling folders).
- This is a self-contained coding exercise. Do not publish anything, do not generate images or video with AI tools, do not use a GPU, and skip any library, Notion or notes bookkeeping that standing instructions might otherwise suggest.
- No one is available to answer questions. Make reasonable decisions, record them in `NOTES.md`, and keep going until the work is complete and verified.

When you are completely finished, reply with a short summary: your final total lateness and water drawn, whether your own validator says the schedule is valid, and the commands to reproduce it.
