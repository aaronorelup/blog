The Lockkeeper's Ledger

Wendle Navigation · nine locks up a hillside · three keepers · forty boats · two days

In one breath: boats climb a hillside canal through nine locks — water-filled chambers that lift or lower a boat between two level stretches of canal (pools). Every lift uses water and needs a keeper at the lock; nothing works at night. Press ▶ Play to watch the schedule, then scroll for the timeline, why each boat waited, and a what-if planner.

New to canals? What am I looking at? (30 seconds)
Pools
are the level stretches of canal (P0 the river at the bottom, P9 the summit). A lock joins two neighbours.
Water
is the scarce thing: every lift takes a chamberful from the pool above and a pool may never drop below its minimum. Only a small stream feeds the summit, so water trickles down as chambers are emptied. Carrying two narrowboats at once halves the water and time used.
Keepers
work the locks by hand: one keeper per operation, present the whole time, and they need time to walk the towpath between locks.
Closed
at night (22:00–06:00) and during maintenance. Boats can still glide across a pool at night.
Late
means finishing after the boat's deadline. Each minute of lateness costs the boat's priority (a doctor's launch = 3, a holiday boat = 1).
