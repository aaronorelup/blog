I was talking to a friend who got into AI about a month ago. He's already caught up with me, and
I've been at this for six years. That's honestly the best news I've heard in a while, because it
means **you're never more than a month behind in AI.** Whatever you missed is already gone.

## Six years, mostly gone

I've been into AI since it first came out, about six years now. I obsessed over it. I ran local
models on my own computer and tried pretty much everything. Then I started using Claude Code with
the newer Claude models and things opened up. I started making all the stuff on this blog, and
now AI is basically my whole life. I spend my time researching it and trying every new thing.

And honestly, 90 to 95% of what I learned before that is obsolete now. The workarounds, the
tricks, the tools I fought with. None of it matters anymore. If you figure out what's new this
week, you're pretty much caught up.

## How he caught up in a month

He didn't study the history. He looked at what's good right now and set himself up around it.

- He got subscriptions to several AIs, not just one.
- He figured out which model is good at what.
- He uses the one that's best at web search to do his research.
- He uses the one that's best at long agent jobs for those. An agent is an AI that works through
  a task on its own, one step after another, for as long as it takes.
- He uses coding agents for the coding.
- He has an orchestrator, an agent that hands out work to the others, running Claude Code for him.

It's working really well for him. His setup might even be better than mine.

## Where I got it wrong

I haven't changed my setup much this month. I've been moving things off my laptop and onto the
cloud, but I held off on paying for more subscriptions and running several at once. That was
the wrong call. Even on the cheapest plan, the model that's best at the job beats the one you
happen to have open.

I have two of Claude's biggest plans for Claude Code, and nothing running them the way his
orchestrator does. That would probably work really well for me too. So here's my list:

- Actually work my ChatGPT subscription into how I do things.
- Set up a Grok bot.
- Start using Muse.

## Take it easy

I worry a lot about keeping up. I feel like if I stop for a month, I'll be obsolete, and that's
100% true. It's also true the other way around. If I start again a month later, I'll be up and
running again a month after that.

So there's not much reason to worry. However you've done so far, a month is all it takes to
catch up. That's kind of what the tea house is for. Come in, sit down, don't worry. Everything I
learn goes on this blog, and it's yours for free.
